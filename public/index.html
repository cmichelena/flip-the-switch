Flip the
Switch

Remove one thing from the world today. Give it some years to be replaced, or none. Watch what happens to the people, the economy and how long they live over the next century.

The trap is the second slider. Almost everyone knows what they would switch off. Almost nobody has decided how many years to give the replacement.

How it works

The numbers are judgement calls. The mechanisms are the argument. Every switch has four levers you can edit: its long-run effect on fertility, on growth, the shock it causes if removed overnight, and how many years its replacement takes to build.

The shock scales with the gap between the years you give and the build time. Give a switch its full build time and the shock disappears. Give it zero and you get the whole thing. Fertility effects add, growth effects compound, and a world with fewer people grows a smaller economy.

The shaded band is variance, not a forecast range. It widens with every check you remove. Hand government to a machine while removing countries and the band becomes a fork: the model shows the better branch and says so.
